From raw capture to something you can show

A bare screenshot is evidence, not a slide. This page composes it for you: a background behind it — six gradients, plain white, paper, or fully transparent — breathing room around it, gently rounded corners and a believable shadow, letterboxed to square, 4:3 or 16:9 if a deck or a post needs it.

Then you mark it up on top: an arrow to the thing that matters, a box around it, a freehand scribble, a text label. Every control re-renders live, and the export is a clean PNG at normal or double resolution — or straight onto your clipboard.

Blur the parts that are nobody's business

Screenshots leak: an email address in the corner, an account balance, an API token in a terminal. The blur tool takes the region you drag and pixelates it — the original pixels are scaled down to coarse blocks and written back, so the detail is destroyed in the exported image itself, not hidden under an overlay someone could peel off.

Be generous with the box. Pixelation destroys what it covers, but a value that pokes out past the edge is still readable — and as with any redaction, blur the whole value, not just most of it.

The blocks are deliberately coarse, never smaller than 12 pixels, because fine pixelation of text can be partly reversed by tools that match it against rendered letters. For a password or a key, crop it out entirely: nothing beats not having the pixels at all.

Questions

Is my screenshot uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is opened, composed and exported by your own browser, on your own machine. No request carries it, nothing is stored, and the page loads no third-party code at all — the only network activity is an anonymous page-view count.

How do I blur sensitive information?

Pick the Blur tool and drag a box over the part to hide — an email address, a balance, a token. That region is genuinely pixelated: the original pixels are replaced by coarse blocks before export, not covered by a removable overlay. Draw the box generously so nothing legible survives at the edges.

Why is there no watermark?

Because there is nothing to pay for. The work happens on your own machine, so it costs nothing to run and there is nothing to meter, no account to push you toward, and no reason to stamp your image.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The page uses no libraries; the only thing it loads from elsewhere is an anonymous page-view counter, and your image never goes anywhere. Once it is open it works with the connection switched off. Everything — compositing, annotation, blur, export — is plain code running in your browser.

What image formats can I use?

PNG, JPEG and WebP go in — pasted with Ctrl+V, dropped onto the page, or picked as a file. The result comes out as a PNG at normal or double resolution, or is copied straight to the clipboard.

Can it read the text out of a screenshot?

Not this page — this one is for making screenshots presentable. Snip2Text extracts the text from an image, also entirely in your browser, and Snip2Field does it from any pixels on your Windows desktop with a hotkey.
